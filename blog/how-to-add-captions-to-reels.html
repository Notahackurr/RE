How to add captions to reels: pick the right route, then make them readable

By J.P. Gentry · 25 November 2026 · 8 min read

If your reel only works when someone can hear it, you are asking the viewer to do something before they get your point. Captions remove that ask, and a lot of people scroll with the sound off.

Adding captions is not one job. There are three routes, and they behave differently once the video leaves your phone. Most of the trouble comes from picking the wrong one.

The three routes, and what each one actually gives you

The platform's own auto captions

Instagram, TikTok and YouTube all generate captions for you. The important thing to know is that on most of these platforms the captions are a separate track that the viewer switches on or off. They are not part of the picture.

Two consequences follow. A viewer who turned captions off once will not see yours. And if someone downloads or reposts your reel, the captions do not come with it, because there was nothing in the file to download.

These tracks are good for accessibility on the platform itself and cost you no work. They are not good if the text is doing real work in your video.

The in-app caption sticker

Instagram's Captions sticker transcribes your audio while you are making the reel and lays the words on the video. You can edit the words, change the style and colour, and drag the block somewhere else in the frame.

Because the sticker sits on the video, it travels with the post in a way the feed track does not. What you do not get is control over the look beyond the sticker's own options.

Captions burned into the file

Burning captions in means the words are part of the picture, exported with the video. Every platform shows them, every download carries them, every repost keeps them, and the font, size, colour and position are yours.

The tradeoff is real: burned-in captions cannot be switched off, resized by the viewer, or read by a screen reader the way a caption track can. If accessibility is the priority, the right answer is often both. A platform track for accessibility, plus burned-in text for the version that gets forwarded.

For a talking-head reel posted to two or three places, burning them in is usually the least work overall, because you caption once instead of three times in three apps.

Which route fits your reel

Ask where the video is going and who needs to see the words.

  • One platform only, no reposting, and you will review the track: the platform's own captions are enough, and they are better for accessibility than text baked into the frame.
  • One platform, but the opening line depends on the text being visible: use the sticker or burn them in. A hook that needs a toggle to work is not a hook.
  • The same reel going to two or more platforms, or into a client's folder: burn them in. One file, captions intact.
  • A language the platform does not caption well: burn them in.

Platforms change these settings every few months, from supported languages to whether captions start on by default. Check the help page for the app you post to rather than trusting a guide, this one included, to be current in a year.

What makes captions readable

One idea per line

A caption line of nine or ten words becomes a block the eye has to solve. Two to four words at a time reads in one glance. If a sentence does not fit that shape, break it, or cut words until it does.

This is why captioning verbatim is often worse than captioning the meaning. Filler and false starts are things we say and do not need to read.

Highlight one word per line

Colouring one word per line gives the eye an anchor and shows which word carries the stress. Highlight more than one and nothing is emphasised. Highlight the word the sentence lands on, not the first one.

Reading speed beats timing

A caption that changes every half second feels frantic, and a viewer who falls behind stops reading and scrolls. Fewer words held slightly longer is easier to follow than many words flicking past.

Keep clear of the platform's furniture

The bottom of a vertical frame belongs to the platform: the caption, the account name, the buttons. The top has its own overlays. Captions parked low get covered up, which is the most common caption mistake in a finished reel.

Watch the completed reel once on the phone you post from, not just in the editor preview. If you cannot read your own captions in the app, neither can anyone else.

Check names, numbers and anything with a proper noun

Speech recognition is good at ordinary sentences and weaker on names, brands, place names and numbers. Those are the words that matter most and the ones a viewer will notice going wrong.

Read the captions once asking one question: is there anything here that sounds like me but is not what I said. Fix it before you export.

Four failures worth checking for

Two caption systems at once. You burn captions in, then leave the platform's auto captions switched on, and the two sets of words sit on top of each other. If the text is already in the picture, turn the platform track off for that post.

Captions over a face. The text lands across the mouth or the eyes, so the viewer splits attention between the words and the expression. Move the block, or reframe so there is room for both.

Captions the same colour as the wall behind you. A caption that reads fine on one take disappears on the next. A solid background bar or a strong outline fixes it.

Captions that are technically right and still unreadable. Correct words, sensible timing, a font size that only works on a laptop.

A caption check before you post

  1. Watch the finished file on a phone with the sound off. If you cannot follow the point, the captions are not working yet.
  2. Read every proper noun, brand name and number against what you actually said.
  3. Look at the top and bottom of the frame and confirm nothing important is hidden by the app's interface.
  4. Play it once at normal speed and check you can finish each line before it changes.
  5. Turn the platform's auto captions off if your text is already in the picture.
  6. If the reel is going anywhere other than one platform, confirm the captions are in the exported file.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use Instagram's auto captions or add my own?

Use Instagram's own captions when the reel stays on Instagram and you will review the text. Add your own, with the sticker or burned into the file, when the opening line depends on the text being visible, or when the same reel goes to another platform. The platform track is a viewer toggle; text in the picture is not.

Do burned-in captions hurt reach?

None of the big three platforms stops you posting a video with text already in the picture. What matters is that the words are readable and correct. If you burn captions in, switch the platform's auto captions off for that post so the two do not overlap.

Why do my captions get cut off at the bottom of the reel?

Because the app's interface sits over the bottom of a vertical video: the caption, the account name and the buttons. Keep the caption block out of the lower part of the frame, and check the finished reel inside the app rather than only in your editor's preview.

How many words should be on screen at once?

Enough to be a phrase, not enough to be a sentence. Two to four words is a practical range for a vertical frame. Beyond that the viewer is reading while also trying to watch you, and gives up on one of the two.

Can I edit the captions after they are made?

In Reel Studio every caption line appears in the dashboard before you export, so words can be corrected and styling changed. On the platforms it varies: some let you edit an auto-generated track after posting and some do not, and burned-in text cannot be changed without re-exporting. Correct them before you publish.
